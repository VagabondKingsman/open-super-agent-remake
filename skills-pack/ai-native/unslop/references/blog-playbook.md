Read at Step 1 of the brief-to-post workflow. Pick the post type, then use its skeleton as a starting shape, not a form to fill. Drop any part the content doesn't need.

## 1. Brief template

When the user asks how to write a good brief, or when a brief is too thin, offer this template. They can fill only the lines they know.

```
Chủ đề:
Người đọc là ai (nghề, trình độ, đang gặp vấn đề gì):
Sau khi đọc, họ cần biết hoặc làm được gì:
Góc nhìn / quan điểm riêng của tôi:
Số liệu, ví dụ, câu chuyện thật tôi có:
Từ khóa SEO chính (nếu có):
Độ dài mong muốn:
Giọng văn (thân thiện / chuyên gia / bán hàng / kỹ thuật):
Lời kêu gọi cuối bài (đăng ký, mua, đọc bài khác, liên hệ):
Website / thương hiệu, tiếng Anh kiểu Mỹ hay Anh:
```

The two most valuable lines are the reader and the real examples. A post with real examples beats a longer post without them.

## 2. Defaults when the brief is silent

| Missing | Default | Say so in "Giả định" |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 900-1,300 words for how-to and opinion; 600-900 for news; 1,500+ only if asked | yes |
| Reader | Smart non-expert who has the problem the topic solves | yes |
| Spelling | American English | yes |
| Voice | Second person ("you"), conversational expert, contractions on | only if unusual |
| CTA | A soft next step related to the topic, or a placeholder `[add your CTA]` | yes |
| Keyword | None; skip keyword-placement rules, still give title and meta | yes |

## 3. Post types and skeletons

### How-to / tutorial
1. Opening: the result the reader will get and who it's for (2-3 sentences).
2. What you need (only if there are real prerequisites).
3. Steps as H2s phrased as actions ("Export the data as CSV"), each with the why in one line and a concrete detail (command, setting, screenshot placeholder).
4. Common mistakes or troubleshooting (optional).
5. Next step.

### Listicle ("7 ways to…")
1. Opening: the problem and the selection criterion ("These are the ones that worked for teams under 10 people").
2. Items as H2s that make a claim, each with one example. Use the natural number of items, not a round one.
3. How to choose between them (short).
4. Next step.

### Opinion / thought leadership
1. Opening: the claim, stated plainly in the first paragraph.
2. Why the common view is wrong or incomplete.
3. Evidence: two or three sections, each one reason with an example.
4. The strongest objection and an honest answer.
5. What the reader should do differently.

### Product / feature announcement
1. Opening: what's new and who it helps, in one or two sentences.
2. The problem it fixes, from the user's side.
3. How it works (short, with a screenshot placeholder).
4. Availability, price, limits (exact).
5. How to try it.

### Case study
1. Opening: the client or project, the problem, the result with its number.
2. Situation before.
3. What was done, in order, with the decisions and why.
4. Results (only numbers from the brief).
5. What others can take from it.

### Comparison ("X vs Y")
1. Opening: the verdict for the most common reader, up front.
2. Comparison table on the criteria that matter.
3. One section per important difference.
4. "Choose X if… / choose Y if…".

## 4. Titles and headings

Good title patterns:
- The result plus the reader: "How small teams cut onboarding time in half".
- The specific claim: "Your landing page is too long".
- The number plus the specific: "5 pricing mistakes we made in our first year".
- The question the reader types: "How much does a WordPress site cost in Vietnam?"

Avoid: "Ultimate guide", "Everything you need to know", "Unlocking", "Mastering", "The power of", "Revolutionize", "A deep dive", colons stacked with buzzwords ("X: The Key to Unlocking Y").

H2s carry claims or actions. Readers who only skim the H2s should get the post's argument.

## 5. SEO pack

- **Title tag:** 60 characters or fewer, keyword near the start, reads like a human headline.
- **Meta description:** 155 characters or fewer, one or two sentences, says what the reader gets, includes the keyword once, no "In this article".
- **Slug:** lowercase, hyphens, 3-6 words, keyword included, no stop words if avoidable.
- **Keyword placement:** title, first 100 words, one H2, meta. After that, natural use only.
- **Secondary keywords:** 2-3 related phrases a searcher would also type. Use them where they fit, never in a list for their own sake.
- **Internal links:** suggest `[link to: …]` placeholders where the site likely has a related post.

## 6. Pre-delivery checklist

- The post's point fits in one sentence, and the opening states it.
- Every number, quote, study, and story comes from the brief or is a placeholder.
- The scanner reports "clean", or each remaining flag is a deliberate choice.
- Articles, plurals and tense checked (T5-T7).
- No section repeats another's point.
- The ending gives one next step.
- The Vietnamese summary matches the English, section for section.
